# Decisions: Delano User Value and Product Quality Gates

## Active Decisions

- D-001 (accepted): Treat this as a Mode 4, high-risk, planned project because the future change crosses lifecycle contracts, skills, CLI/state transitions, validation, supervision UI, closeout, fixtures, generated runtime, and package boundaries.
- D-002 (accepted): Require a prototype probe before activation. The probe must test behavior against the Kotlin regression and valid controls; source inspection alone does not prove the intervention resists eager-agent gaming.
- D-003 (accepted): Preserve the core `boil-it-down` insight but do not require a standalone `payoff.md`. User/job/capability/demo truth belongs in the spec; vertical proof and proportionality belong in the plan; demo traceability belongs in tasks; concise decisions/evidence belong in existing updates.
- D-004 (accepted): Use a layered gate. Deterministic checks enforce structure and lifecycle, short skills enforce confrontation and stopping, behavioral evaluations test agent decisions, and humans judge the running product.
- D-005 (accepted): Apply the full value contract to new modes 2 through 4. Keep modes 0 and 1 light for genuinely bounded work, add an escalation invariant for new multi-step capability/material uncertainty/cross-surface coordination, and test split-task mode laundering.
- D-006 (accepted): Classify meaningful work as `product`, `infrastructure`, or `mixed` and declare a `none|ui|cli|api|workflow|mixed` human-facing surface profile consistent with upstream intent/touchpoints. Product and mixed scopes owe a representative demo and applicable product review. Infrastructure owes an explicit zero-new-user-capability disclosure, first felt-value boundary, technical proof, and owner acceptance.
- D-007 (accepted): Use stable `DEMO-###` steps tagged `job-critical` or `supporting` as the value trace. Task or evidence completion is not user-value progress; only representative execution can change progress, and a supporting step cannot satisfy a boundary expecting a critical step.
- D-008 (accepted): Require the smallest state-action-result job loop and human product-shape review before broad invisible work. Every foundation slice must enable named demo steps with a proportionality rationale or be honestly separated as infrastructure.
- D-009 (accepted): Trigger re-boil at declared value-risk boundaries and workstream handoffs, and immediately on detected scope mismatch. Each boundary names expected critical steps; reject a fixed number of tasks, hours, files, or updates as the primary trigger.
- D-010 (accepted): Give every executor and supervisor authority and an obligation to stop when scope cannot deliver the intended job or when material foundations advance without working demo progress. An unresolved `reshape-required` or `stop` decision blocks unrelated starts.
- D-011 (accepted): Product closeout requires the literal representative demo on representative real data plus named human judgment of job completion, information architecture, copy, contextual actions, data usefulness, and interaction quality. Automated evidence supports but cannot replace that decision.
- D-012 (accepted): Keep screenshots and recordings optional evidence. Do not use word bans, line caps, universal UX scores, task/file-count limits, or update deletion as product-value enforcement.
- D-013 (accepted): Make the failed Kotlin foundation a formal three-gate regression with cosmetic, schema-complete, and screenshot-complete gaming variants. Add honest-infrastructure, useful-product, small-mode, and legacy controls to measure false blocking.
- D-014 (accepted): Version adoption explicitly. New meaningful projects opt in; planned legacy projects migrate before activation; active projects migrate at the next value boundary and cannot silently close; terminal history remains unchanged.
- D-015 (accepted): Keep the Viewer change minimal but required. Operators must be able to see classification, working demo progress, current value decision, blockers, and required human action without mistaking task totals for payoff.
- D-016 (accepted): The value contract may not redefine an upstream job downward to make a demo pass. Moving an essential capability to non-capabilities requires an owner-approved outcome change or honest infrastructure reclassification.

## Superseded Decisions

- The session proposal's mandatory `payoff.md` is superseded by D-003 because a parallel artifact can become another self-contained compliance target.
- The proposal's 30-line cap, banned-word list, screenshot requirement, and one-update-file limit are superseded by D-009/D-011/D-012. They may inspire concise UX but are not evidence that the user job works.
- The initial idea of treating every foundation-heavy project as a product failure is superseded by D-006. Infrastructure is valid when its lack of immediate user payoff and first felt-value boundary are explicit and approved.

## Open Decision Questions

- OD-001: What approval provenance is sufficient? Recommendation: require a named owner or delegated human decision bound to the current spec/demo content hash, while documenting that local files provide accountability rather than authenticated identity.
- OD-002: What exact value-risk-boundary syntax should plans use? Recommendation: stable named checkpoints declared in the plan and required at each workstream handoff, with event-driven stop authority available at any time.
- OD-003: May an infrastructure project activate when its first felt-value project is named but not yet approved? Recommendation: yes only with explicit owner acceptance of that gap; the project must remain classified as infrastructure and cannot claim product success.
- OD-004: Who may conduct product review? Recommendation: the owner or a delegated human, never the implementing agent; require a non-implementing reviewer for high-risk work when one is available and allow owner review at solo scale.
- OD-005: Which compact update/schema shape best binds activation, checkpoint, and product-review decisions without duplicating source truth? Resolve through the required prototype before spec activation.
